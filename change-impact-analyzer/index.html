Change Impact Analyzer

Know what your Salesforce change will break before you make it.

Map dependencies, identify downstream risk, and understand what needs to be tested before your next Salesforce change reaches production. A field may feed a Flow. That Flow may trigger Apex. Apex may update another system. The analyzer traces those relationships so you see the blast radius before it becomes a production problem.

Common signs you need it

If your team says things like this, the blast radius is hidden.

“I'm afraid to touch this.”

Does anything still use this field? What is this Flow connected to? Every deployment feels risky because no one is sure.

“We changed one thing and three others broke.”

A seemingly minor change takes down an automation, a report, or an integration, and the troubleshooting eats the rest of the week.

“The person who built this is gone.”

The org has been customized for years, the documentation is stale, and too much of how it works lives only in someone's memory.

The honest FAQ

How is this different from a dependency checker?

A dependency checker answers “what references this component?” The Change Impact Analyzer goes further: what depends on those dependencies, which business processes are involved, how significant the change is, what the blast radius is, and what you should test.

Doesn't Salesforce already show dependencies?

In places, one component at a time, and it misses plenty. Understanding the full technical and business impact usually means checking multiple tools, metadata, and codebases. This connects the full chain and tells you what the risk actually is.

Is it useful even if we're not changing anything?

Yes. It accelerates technical discovery in an unfamiliar org, surfaces technical debt and fragile automation chains, and helps you decide what is safe to clean up.

How is the price set?

By org size and complexity: automation volume, code, integrations. You get the number before we start, and it does not move.
